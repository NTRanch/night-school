# Plan 19 — Pass 2 Technique Sheet: the variation grammar

Distilled from OBSERVATION.md alone. The plan's question,
answered head-on first (§1), then the grammar (§2), the
duration economics (§3), the invariants (§4), and the
automation split (§5).

## §1 Rules or taste? — the verdict

**Rules — with one named taste residue.** The session's
variation method decomposes into eight director-applicable
rules (§2), each with a trigger, a change, and magnitude/
frequency evidence from the event log. The rules are not
inferred from the finished shot (which this artefact never
displays — OBSERVATION U-1) but from the creator's
*working* variation: how a single act is kept alive across
a 19:37 sitting by rotating what is worked, in what order,
with what review instruments. That is the variation craft
a scene director actually applies when building a
sustained scene, and it transfers as rules because every
rule's trigger is a state of the work (a family not yet
passed, a span not yet reviewed at an endpoint), not a
judgement of taste.

The taste residue, named honestly: **which family comes
next** at each rotation point, and **when a family is
"done"**, are not rule-governed in the evidence — no
narration in the transcribed spans states an ordering
principle, and the observed order (flex → pelvis → feet →
fingers → feet-again) does not repeat a pattern the
record can certify. §6 files the grammar's example index
for exactly that residue: the timestamps a director
studies to acquire the ordering taste by example. The
grammar is therefore **rules for the machinery, an
annotated example index for the sequencing taste** — and
the record says why: sequencing is the one decision the
session displays without ever explaining.

## §2 The grammar — eight rules

Each rule: **trigger → change → evidence** (event IDs from
OBSERVATION §3; frequencies per sitting unless noted).

- **V-1 ROTATE BY CHANNEL FAMILY.** *Trigger:* the current
  family's pass stops producing visible change.
  *Change:* put the family down whole; take up a different
  family (rig group, flex family, finger family) and work
  it across the shot's full span before rotating again.
  *Evidence:* 11 family passes (9 POSE + 2 CHANNEL) in
  19.62 min — one pass per ~1.8 min of session; no family
  is worked in two adjacent observation windows except
  across the refinement break. The unit of variation is
  the family pass, never the individual key.
- **V-2 WHOLE-SPAN SELECTION.** *Trigger:* every family
  pass. *Change:* the time selection covers the whole shot
  (≈0–20 s green span) while a family is worked — variation
  is applied across the take, not at a favourite moment.
  *Evidence:* whole-span Motion Editor selections at 245,
  1125 and in the tail sheet; whole-shot selection is the
  session's default state (Plan 18: same).
- **V-3 ENDPOINT REVIEW.** *Trigger:* a pass completes.
  *Change:* review at the shot's endpoints (playhead
  0.000 / 20.000) and by camera flight — never by playing
  the take through. *Evidence:* every legible playhead
  readout in 84 stills is an endpoint; zero playback
  windows in the full energy record (OBSERVATION §4).
- **V-4 RETURN TO SMALL PARTS LATE.** *Trigger:* the large
  families (pelvis, body) are in place. *Change:* re-pass
  the small-part families (feet, fingers) a second/third
  time against the accumulated pose. *Evidence:* feet
  passed at ~267–315 (partner) and again ~1035 (leader);
  fingers at ~645 (graph), ~945, ~1145 — 5 of the 11
  family passes are returns.
- **V-5 CUT LENGTH BEFORE POLISH.** *Trigger:* the rig's
  evaluation cost makes the long form unworkable — here,
  IK-rig lag, worst on the Unknown-section controls
  (narration, 1:06–1:31). *Change:* cut the shot's length
  early and on the record (60.000 → 20.000, a 3× cut,
  decided at ~1:35 of the sitting: "I'll make this a
  20 second animation") and let every later pass work
  the short form. *Evidence:* E-05 + C-03; the 20 s form
  is confirmed at 225/495/566/1160 and is the form Plans
  15/18's sittings share. The rule's mechanism is
  economic, not aesthetic: length is cut to what the
  tool can evaluate interactively, before any polish is
  spent on the long form.
- **V-6 DIAGNOSTIC MODE SURVEY.** *Trigger:* a lighting/
  form question the normal view can't answer. *Change:*
  switch the work viewport to a diagnostic shading mode
  (flat white unlit) and fly the scene in it; return to
  the lit view to judge. *Evidence:* E-11 (388–404 s, the
  file's only sustained white-mode window).
- **V-7 STILL-RENDER CHECKPOINT.** *Trigger:* a pose/
  lighting state worth judging at final quality.
  *Change:* stop posing; run a progressive-refinement
  still at delivery-class settings (DOF override raised
  to 1024 samples; AO mode AO Only) and inspect the
  parked frame. *Evidence:* E-12/E-13 (477–563 s, 7.3% of
  the sitting; status "Sample: 413 of 1024" at 546 s).
- **V-8 POSE IDEA FIRST.** *Trigger:* session start.
  *Change:* state the pose idea before touching the rig —
  the sitting executes a preconceived tableau and varies
  the *working*, not the destination. *Evidence:*
  [SPEECH, slice A stream] "we can get to posing — I have
  a specific pose idea, as I already mentioned"; the
  shot-time-0 tableau is recognisably the same contact
  arrangement at 566, 1125 and 1160 s despite 19 minutes
  of intervening passes.

## §3 Duration economics (from OBSERVATION §3 tallies)

- **Rate:** 23 distinct events / 19.62 min = **11.7
  events per 10 min** (class tags: POSE 9, CAMERA 4,
  FINISH 3, CHANNEL 2, EDITOR 2, CARD 2, LENGTH 1,
  TIME 1, DWELL 1).
- **Where they cluster:** there is no climax cluster —
  POSE events distribute across the full sitting
  (245…1145 s); FINISH events bracket the middle
  (477–563) and the close (1155–1170); the LENGTH event
  is early only; the session's highest screen energy is
  an editor operation (E-06, 211 s), not a scene peak.
  In director terms: **variation effort is flat across
  the duration; finishing effort is pulsed, mid + end.**
- **What a long take costs, in this evidence:** a 20 s
  shot absorbed ≥3 sittings in one day (Plans 15/19/18,
  taskbar 11:40 → 12:19 across instalments) — the
  duration problem the plan names is solved by the
  creator with *more passes over the same span*, not
  with more span.

## §4 The invariants (what NEVER changes — half the grammar)

Across all 84 stills and both energy records:

1. **The session and shot:** `tutorial.dmx`, `shot1`, one
   clip on the timeline (after E-05), no second shot, no
   internal cut — zero scene cuts in 35,311 frames.
2. **The cast and set:** `bonnie_male1`, `bonnie1`,
   `camera1`/`camera2`; the FNAF hallway; nothing enters
   or leaves the element list in any sampled frame.
3. **The act and blocking:** one act, one arrangement;
   the contact tableau at shot-time 0 is the same
   arrangement at every inspection.
4. **The layout:** two viewports (work camera + shot
   camera) for the entire sitting; the shot camera's
   framing is on screen during every posing pass.
5. **The playhead discipline:** parked at an endpoint
   (0.000 or 20.000) in every legible readout; never
   playing.
6. **The tool:** SFM throughout — no second application
   appears in this instalment's sampled frames.
7. **The wrapper shape:** credit at the open, card at
   the close, session in between (cf. Plan 18's longer
   card sequence — the wrapper is a series invariant
   with instalment-scale variation).

A director applying §2 without §4 gets motion without
identity: the grammar's aliveness comes from variation
*inside* a frame that does not move.

## §5 Automation vs hand-keying split

- **Hand-keyed: everything that moves.** All posing is
  gizmo work in the Motion Editor and dense-key work in
  the Graph Editor (E-15's finger lanes; E-06's dense
  lanes). No procedural pass, script, or generator is
  opened in any sampled frame (C-04).
- **Machine-driven: exactly one process** — the
  progressive refinement render (V-7), which varies
  nothing; it *judges*. The session's automation share of
  authoring is 0%; its automation share of review is the
  refinement still plus the renderer's interpolation
  between dense keys.
- Estate translation of the split: the source's
  authoring is 100% manual *because its variation is
  procedural in the human sense* (V-1 rotation). The
  estate's seeded machinery (D-1072 C3) is the
  mechanisation of exactly this rotation — see the
  mechanism mapping in PASS3-REBUILD.md.
