## Technique distillation (mechanisms)

1. **Song-segment album architecture.** One musical number = one
   self-contained segment with its own cast member, set, and grade;
   a title card opens the album and a credits card (song titles + a
   named post-credit song) closes it. The album is a playlist with
   an editor's hand, not a montage.
2. **Datamosh transitions.** Segment joins in the fast passage are
   datamoshed (macroblock smear, RGB-split) rather than cut or
   dissolved — the corruption IS the transition grammar, and it
   licenses hard changes of cast/set/grade without a visual
   apology.
3. **Grade as segment identity.** Each segment carries a distinct
   colour regime (clean bright, night-warm, sepia for the
   post-credit stretch, chromatic-aberration edges on the Juno
   material). The grade changes exactly when the segment does —
   grade is the segment boundary marker the viewer actually reads.
4. **Diegetic capture overlays.** A camcorder HUD frame (timestamp,
   battery, corner brackets) appears as a segment device — the same
   recorded-performance motivation as Plan 54's in-frame camera
   prop, executed as overlay rather than prop.
5. **Contributor watermark tolerance.** Source watermarks are left
   in-frame; the compilation does not pretend to a single author.
   For estate compilation work the inverse is the lesson:
   provenance marks survive edits unless a work is wholly
   re-rendered.

## Estate routing

Editorial grammar (items 1–3) is the portable yield: it is the
reference form for any estate multi-source compilation (album
architecture, datamosh joins, grade-as-boundary). Items 4–5 are
recorded as confirmations. No assets, models, motion, music, or
text extracted or reused; song titles are named only as census
facts.
